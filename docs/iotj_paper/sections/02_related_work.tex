\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{LoRa RFFI and Deployment Variability}
Recent surveys summarize LoRa RFFI datasets, signal representations, and open challenges~\cite{ahmed2024lora_rffi_survey,xie2024rffi_iot_survey}.
LoRa uses chirp spread spectrum modulation, so received captures contain structured time--frequency patterns that can be exploited for device discrimination~\cite{shen2021lora_spectrogram_cnn,alshawabka2021deeplora}.
The OSU LoRa RFFI dataset and associated studies provide a systematic view of deployment variability, including cross-day, cross-location, cross-configuration, and cross-receiver shifts~\cite{elmaghbub2021lora_wild,hamdaoui2022deeplearning,elmaghbub2021dataset_release}.
These works consistently show that models trained and tested under the same collection setting can perform well, whereas cross-setting evaluation exposes substantial degradation, especially under configuration and receiver changes.
Our work adopts this strict source-domain validation framing rather than random splits that mix days or receivers within training and testing.

\subsection{CNN-IQ and Spectrogram RFFI Baselines}
Deep learning has become the dominant approach for RF device fingerprinting.
Large-scale experimental studies demonstrate that convolutional models can learn discriminative patterns from IQ snapshots and related RF views~\cite{jian2020deeplearning_rf_fingerprinting,sankhe2019oracle}.
Prior RF-specific DNN feature designs further motivate multi-view and spectral representations for device classification~\cite{hamdaoui2020deep_nn_features}.
For LoRa specifically, spectrogram-based CNN classifiers leverage chirp structure and often outperform raw-IQ or FFT-only inputs under their own device collections and evaluation protocols~\cite{shen2021lora_spectrogram_cnn,alshawabka2021deeplora}.
These methods motivate our use of a CNN stem to retain local impairment sensitivity, but they also highlight an important evaluation caveat: prior papers frequently assume different datasets, enrollment rules, or adaptation settings.
Accordingly, we do not claim direct leaderboard comparison against high-accuracy LoRa RFFI systems reported elsewhere.
Instead, we reproduce a CNN-IQ baseline under our unified Day1--3/Day4/Day5 protocol and use it as the primary reference point.

\subsection{OOB Hardware Impairments and Prior OOB Usage}
Transmitter hardware imperfections can appear not only in the in-band LoRa chirp but also in out-of-band spectral leakage and distortion.
WideScan~\cite{elmaghbub2020widescan} and subsequent OSU LoRa work show that OOB views can strengthen device signatures caused by power-amplifier nonlinearity, filter roll-off, and related analog effects~\cite{hamdaoui2022deeplearning}.
IoTJ RFFI studies further demonstrate that robust fingerprint extraction remains important for practical device recognition under noisy or variable conditions~\cite{zhou2021robust_rffi_iotj,xie2018coherent_rff_iotj}.
Because OOB evidence is already established in the LoRa RFFI literature, our contribution is not the first use of OOB information.
Rather, we study \emph{how} OOB evidence should enter the model.
Naive concatenation or static gating can expose the main RF pathway to noisy OOB shortcuts, whereas token-level cross-attention allows main-path RF tokens to selectively query OOB hardware evidence on a per-sample basis.

\subsection{RF Sequence Modeling and Cross-Attention Fusion}
Most CNN baselines operate on fixed-length windows with limited explicit modeling of long-range dependencies across LoRa chirp structure.
Attention-based sequence models provide a flexible alternative for token-level RF representation learning~\cite{vaswani2017attention}.
Our RF-HSTU encoder applies sigmoid-gated token mixing with relative position bias over RF patch tokens, while a separate cross-attention module injects OOB evidence after local CNN-stem encoding.
This ordering preserves local distortion features before global sequence mixing and differs fundamentally from early OOB concatenation, which our ablations show can collapse under cross-day evaluation with only 24 test files per fold.

\subsection{Domain Shift, Metric Learning, and Adaptation}
Cross-day and cross-receiver LoRa RFFI are domain-shift problems.
Confounding factors such as channel, time, and receiver changes can dominate learned fingerprints if not controlled~\cite{cekic2020robust_wireless_fingerprinting,alshawabka2020exposing_fingerprint,shen2022scalable_lora_rffi}.
Recent LoRa RFFI studies explore metric learning, contrastive learning, and channel-independent representations to improve robustness~\cite{shen2022scalable_lora_rffi,ma2025contrastive_lora_rffi,ma2025receiver_independent_rffi}.
Center loss and supervised contrastive learning provide general tools for embedding compactness and class separation~\cite{wen2016center_loss,khosla2020supcon}.
Test-time adaptation methods such as TENT and TTN further examine whether target-domain normalization statistics can recover performance without labels~\cite{wang2021tent,lim2023ttn}.
Prior channel-robust or receiver-oriented LoRa RFFI papers often report high accuracy under different splits and signal views; we explicitly avoid claiming direct comparison with those numbers.
These approaches provide important robustness strategies, but they often involve additional pretraining objectives, metric-learning losses, target-domain adaptation, or protocol-specific enrollment designs.
In contrast, our evaluation focuses on a controlled representation-fusion question under a fixed source-domain validation protocol: how OOB evidence should be injected into RF token representations without using target-domain labels.

\subsection{IoT Physical-Layer Security}
Physical-layer authentication has been surveyed as a complementary security mechanism for wireless networks~\cite{xie2021physical_layer_auth_survey}.
In LoRaWAN-style IoT systems, it can complement cryptographic protections by verifying that the transmitting hardware matches an enrolled device identity~\cite{lorawan_spec,hamdaoui2022deeplearning}.
This is especially relevant for long-lived IoT deployments where credential compromise, device cloning, or unauthorized hardware substitution must be detected at the gateway.
However, physical-layer authentication is useful only if fingerprint models remain stable under realistic deployment shifts~\cite{elmaghbub2021lora_wild}.
Our cross-day, deployment-shift, and cross-receiver results are designed to test that stability boundary rather than same-setting accuracy alone.
